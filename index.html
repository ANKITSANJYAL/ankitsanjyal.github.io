About

I am an interpretability and AI safety researcher (M.S. Data Science, Fordham University). The question I keep coming back to is: when can we say a system's externalized computation is a reliable window into its actual decision process — and what breaks that reliability? Chain-of-thought monitoring is cheap, scalable, and increasingly treated as a first line of defence, but the behavioral results keep showing that written reasoning does not track the features actually driving outputs. I want to work the mechanistic layer underneath those results, and then ask what survives when a system stops being one model and becomes many talking to each other.

My path here runs through hands-on work in model interpretability (extracting and causally intervening on internal representations), causal inference, and building autonomous multi-agent systems, alongside four arXiv preprints and a stint as Graduate Research Assistant in the Educational Data Mining Lab. I completed BlueDot Impact's Technical AI Safety course and now work on interpretability research full-time. I am open to collaboration, mentorship, and fellowship or research roles in the area.

Research Interests

Keywords: Mechanistic Interpretability, Chain-of-Thought Monitoring, AI Safety, Model Internals, Causal Inference, Activation Steering, Agentic Systems, Multi-Agent Safety

I am interested in a question with two halves. When can we say a system's externalized computation is a reliable window into its actual decision process, and what breaks that reliability? CoT monitoring is cheap and scalable, but Turpin, Lanham, and Chen keep showing that the written reasoning does not reflect the features driving the output, and Korbak frames it as a fragile opportunity. I want the mechanistic layer beneath those results. The second half is what happens when systems stop being one model and become many talking to each other — the trace becomes a delegation graph, the messages become a second reasoning channel with its own faithfulness problem, and watching each agent stops adding up to watching the system.

Read the full research statement →

  • Reasoning-model interpretability — which reasoning steps actually drive a model's answer, identified via counterfactual resampling and intervention.
  • Internals-based monitoring — cheap linear probes on activations to detect deception and evaluation-awareness, as a first layer of model control.
  • Automating interpretability — autonomous investigator agents that use interpretability tools to explain why a model behaved a certain way.
  • Latent-reasoning interpretability — keeping models legible as the field moves toward vector- and diffusion-based reasoning that breaks CoT monitoring.
  • Multi-agent monitorability — whether faithfulness survives composition: secret collusion, inter-agent messages as a second externalized channel, and what a faithfulness metric even means once the trace is a delegation graph.

GitHub
CoT Faithfulness Under Necessity vs. Propensity
AI Safety LessWrong

Misleading cues injected into 1,700 problems per model across three families. Where reasoning is computationally necessary, capture is near-zero (0.0–2.2%); where it is optional, cues land up to 41% of the time — and one model disclosed the cue once in 489 captured traces. Necessity protects monitoring by prevention, not disclosure.
