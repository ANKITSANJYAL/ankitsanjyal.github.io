what the model says, and what it actually did

I work on whether a model’s stated reasoning is a reliable window into the computation that actually produced it — and what breaks when one model becomes many talking to each other.

Right now that means chain-of-thought faithfulness: injecting misleading cues into thousands of problems and measuring, trace by trace, whether the reasoning ever admits them. Capture is near zero where reasoning is computationally necessary and reaches 41% where it is optional.

M.S. Data Science at Fordham, graduating May 2026. Before that, two years building data infrastructure in Bangalore, which is where I learned that most of research is plumbing.

I’m looking for interpretability and AI safety work — fellowships, research roles, collaborators.

Multimodal Emotion Recognition via Causal-Diffusion Bridge

2025

A causal graph re-weights text, audio and video; counterfactual sampling makes attribution explicit, so the model reports which modality drove each prediction.

paper ↗ Preprint
A NOTEARS causal graph re-weights the text, audio and video channels, a β-VAE compresses them, and a stop-gradiented 1D DDPM prior structures the latent space. Counterfactual sampling over that graph makes attribution explicit — an 18% relative improvement on CMU-MOSEI, and the only system with non-trivial performance on the minority emotions where every baseline fails.
